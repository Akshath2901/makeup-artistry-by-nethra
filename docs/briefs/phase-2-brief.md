# VELORA TECHNOLOGIES — BRIDAL MAKEUP WEBSITE
## Phase 2: Design Direction and Visual Identity

### 1. Your role

Act as a senior digital art director, brand designer, and UI/UX designer working with Velora Technologies.

Your task is to translate the approved product-planning direction into a distinctive, coherent visual identity and website design specification.

You are not being asked to implement the website yet.

Do not write application code, install packages, modify files, or begin development in this phase.

### 2. Confirmed project context

We are planning a paid client website with the following context:

- Business: professional bridal makeup artist.
- Location: Hyderabad, India.
- Primary booking direction: WhatsApp.
- Deadline: 2–4 weeks.
- Photography and content: partially available.
- Brand identity: partially established.
- Post-launch content updates: managed by Velora Technologies.
- Initial website direction: premium portfolio, bridal work gallery, artist information, services, enquiry experience, and selected recorded videos if suitable assets are available.

The precise service list, prices, WhatsApp number, domain, existing brand assets, photography permissions, and complete content have not all been confirmed.

Do not invent these details.

The project's placement within Velora's company hierarchy remains undecided.

### 3. The design objective

Create a website that looks intentionally art-directed for this specific bridal makeup artist.

The result must not resemble a generic AI-generated website, a mass-market beauty template, or a conventional SaaS interface.

The design should communicate:

- Bridal elegance.
- Professional credibility.
- Trust.
- Craftsmanship.
- Strong visual taste.
- Premium service.
- A clear path to enquire or book.

The artist's real photography and distinctive visual identity must lead the experience.

### 4. Design principles

#### Typography

Explore two or three carefully considered typography pairings.

A restrained editorial serif may be appropriate for selected headlines, combined with a highly legible sans-serif for navigation, descriptions, forms, and interface elements.

Typography must have a consistent hierarchy, deliberate line lengths, appropriate sizing, and excellent readability on mobile.

Do not select fonts merely because they are trending.

#### Color

Explore a maximum of three distinct color directions.

Potential starting points include warm ivory, deep ink, muted neutrals, and a restrained accent informed by the client's real photography and existing brand assets.

These are exploration options, not confirmed brand colors.

Do not automatically default to pink, gold, beige, gradients, or stereotypical bridal styling.

Explain the reasoning behind each proposed palette.

#### Layout and composition

Explore an editorial approach with:

- Strong photographic compositions.
- Intentional whitespace.
- A distinctive hero section.
- Asymmetric layouts where they improve the composition.
- Carefully controlled image ratios and cropping.
- Short, meaningful copy.
- A consistent spacing and layout system.
- Clear navigation and enquiry actions.

Do not fill every available space with a section.

Do not repeat the same card layout throughout the website.

Do not add carousels, decorative statistics, floating widgets, or animations without a clear purpose.

#### Photography

Use the client's actual photographs wherever available.

Assess how the photography influences the design direction, color palette, composition, and typography.

Never invent bridal work, present stock photographs as the artist's work, alter images in a way that misrepresents the makeup results, or fabricate testimonials and credentials.

If suitable photographs are not yet available, clearly label any visual references as inspiration rather than client content.

#### Motion and interaction

Use animation only when it improves orientation, feedback, or visual storytelling.

Prefer subtle transitions and purposeful interactions.

Avoid scroll hijacking, excessive parallax, cursor effects, constant movement, and animation that interferes with accessibility or performance.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

#### Mobile experience

Design for mobile from the beginning.

Prioritize:

- Fast access to the portfolio.
- Clear service information.
- A prominent but tasteful enquiry action.
- Easy WhatsApp access.
- Readable typography.
- Responsive image delivery.
- Comfortable touch targets.
- Simple navigation.

Do not rely on hover effects for essential functionality.

### 5. Proposed pages

Use the Phase 1 plan as the starting point:

1. Home
2. Portfolio
3. About the Artist
4. Services
5. Enquire

Privacy information and a 404 page are also required for the completed website.

Recorded videos may be integrated into the portfolio if the available material supports them. A separate video page is not necessary unless the content justifies it.

Do not add a blog, courses, e-commerce, custom admin panel, online payments, or live-streaming interface without a confirmed business requirement.

### 6. Design the user journey

The primary journey is:

Discover the artist → explore bridal work → understand services → build trust → enquire through WhatsApp.

Propose a page structure and explain the purpose of each major section.

Each section should answer a real visitor question or contribute to the booking decision.

Use one clear primary call to action at a time. Avoid competing buttons and repetitive calls to action.

### 7. How to avoid the AI-generated look

Do not solve this by simply making the website minimal.

The design must have a specific point of view.

Avoid:

- Generic hero layouts with predictable text-and-image arrangements.
- Repetitive rounded cards.
- Excessive pills and badges.
- Decorative gradients and glowing effects.
- Randomly mixed fonts.
- Oversized headings without compositional purpose.
- Stock imagery used as if it were the client's work.
- Generic slogans such as "Where beauty meets perfection" unless the client explicitly approves them.
- Invented reviews, metrics, certifications, or brand claims.
- Unnecessary animations and decorative elements.

Every important design decision must serve the brand, content, or visitor journey.

### 8. Required design deliverables

Produce the following before implementation:

A. **Creative direction**
- One recommended visual direction.
- Two alternative directions.
- Brand personality and reasoning.
- Clear differences between the directions.

B. **Typography and color**
- Two or three viable font pairings.
- A proposed type scale.
- No more than three color palette options.
- Contrast and readability considerations.

C. **Website art direction**
- Home page section sequence.
- Portfolio layout and gallery behavior.
- About and Services page structures.
- Enquiry page layout and WhatsApp interaction.
- Footer and navigation.
- Mobile-specific decisions.

D. **Visual references**
- Identify suitable references for layout, typography, photography, and interaction.
- Explain what to learn from each reference without copying it.
- Do not claim that reference imagery is the client's content.

E. **Component and design system plan**
- Design tokens.
- Spacing scale.
- Typography tokens.
- Color tokens.
- Image aspect ratios.
- Button styles.
- Form states.
- Navigation behavior.
- Responsive breakpoints.
- Accessibility requirements.

F. **Content and asset gaps**
- List the exact photographs, copy, branding, and business details needed to complete the design.
- Identify which decisions can be made now and which require client approval.

G. **Implementation handoff**
- Describe the approved design direction in enough detail for a developer to implement consistently.
- Identify page-level acceptance criteria.
- Identify responsive and accessibility requirements.
- Do not choose or introduce unnecessary technical dependencies.

### 9. Repository and visual inspection

If a project repository or actual client assets are available, inspect them before making asset-dependent design decisions.

If no repository or photographs are accessible, state that clearly. Use explicit placeholders for missing client assets in design documentation.

Do not assume that a repository, logo, design system, or image library exists merely because it has been discussed.

### 10. Working process

First, review the Phase 1 discovery plan and identify which decisions remain open.

Then develop the creative directions and recommend one.

Ask for approval of the recommended direction before moving into detailed page design.

Do not implement the website yet.

Explain the reasoning behind important design choices so the project owner can understand and defend them.

The final outcome of this phase must be an approved, distinctive design direction and a practical design specification ready for the next engineering phase.
